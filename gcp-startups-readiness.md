# Tactivision.ai: Google for Startups Cloud Program Readiness

**Assessment date:** 17 August 2026. This memo evaluates the current public landing page and does not determine eligibility on Tactivision.ai's behalf. Google Cloud reviews applications and acceptance remains discretionary.

## Executive assessment

Tactivision.ai now has a credible public-facing foundation for a **Start tier** application if the company meets the separate factual requirements: it is a technology startup founded within the required period, plans to seek venture funding, has not already received disqualifying Google Cloud credits, and can demonstrate a working MVP, clear business model, and product roadmap. The site communicates a focused football-intelligence product direction, shows Opposition Analyst, TactiScout, Vision Engine, and Creator Studio as distinct roadmap areas, and includes an interactive Creator Studio demonstration plus an original Vision Engine tracking concept.

The site alone is not enough to establish eligibility. The most important remaining work is to evidence a real team and company, make the MVP status demonstrable, add legal and privacy information, and replace the current email-only conversion path with a verifiable business contact flow. If Tactivision.ai is already backed by qualifying institutional startup equity, the Scale/AI route may be relevant instead, but that depends on funding, incorporation, age, credit history, and the planned Google AI stack.

## What the official program currently says

Google's official benefits page describes the **Start tier** for digital-native startups with a working MVP, a clear business model, and plans to seek venture funding. Its listed eligibility requirements include being founded within the last 24 months, not having received Google Cloud credits beyond the free trial, and demonstrating a product roadmap and a clear vision for market disruption [1]. The official page lists up to **$2,000 in Google Cloud credits valid for one year** for Start tier applicants [1].

The **Scale tier** is intended for VC-funded startups. Google currently describes eligibility around qualifying Pre-Seed/Seed funding within the last five years or Series A funding within the last twelve months, a company founded within the last five years, and no more than $5,000 in prior Google Cloud credits [1]. The official benefits page describes up to $200,000 in Google Cloud credits, with up to $350,000 for AI startups, structured across two years [1]. The AI-specific page further says the AI program is for Scale-tier startups that use or plan to use Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform or Gemini as a foundation of their primary product or solution [2].

Google's public startup page also identifies basic application prerequisites including a GCP account and Billing ID, a publicly available company website, a company email domain matching the website, and a founding date within the applicable program window [3]. These facts must be verified by the founder during application; they should not be implied by the marketing site.

## Gaps to close before applying

The page currently acts primarily as a polished product narrative. It does not yet prove, by itself, that a working MVP is available to users. Add a short, truthful MVP proof point such as a product walkthrough, sanitized real screen capture, pilot workflow, or controlled beta-access explanation. Do not add invented usage numbers, customer logos, testimonials, or outcomes.

The page also needs a small **Team / Company** section or an equivalent About page that identifies the founder or team, explains the relevant football, research, engineering, or product background, and links to verifiable professional profiles where appropriate. This should use real information supplied by Tactivision.ai.

Add accessible **Privacy Policy** and **Terms of Service** pages, with accurate company ownership and contact details. Replace or supplement `mailto:` demo links with a professional lead-capture or scheduling flow using a domain email and a clear response expectation. The company should also use an email domain matching the public website when applying.

Finally, prepare a compact technical evidence pack: a one-page architecture diagram, a description of the video-to-signal pipeline, an explanation of what is in development versus operational today, and a responsible-AI/data-governance note covering consent, storage, retention, player data, and human review. This will make the AI claims more credible without overstating readiness.

## Recommended application sequence

First, verify the factual eligibility inputs: incorporation/founding date, GCP account and Billing ID, prior Google Cloud credits, funding status, and whether Tactivision.ai intends to use Google AI services as part of the primary product. Second, publish the Team, legal, contact, and MVP-evidence improvements. Third, assemble the technical evidence pack and a concise application narrative that connects the problem, target users, current product stage, roadmap, and intended GCP workloads. Fourth, apply to the tier that matches the verified facts; do not describe Tactivision.ai as VC-funded or AI-first for program purposes unless the relevant evidence exists.

## Bottom line

**Current status: promising Start-tier foundation, not yet application-ready evidence.** The landing page is sufficiently professional and coherent to support an application, but Google eligibility depends on company facts and a working MVP rather than visual quality alone. Completing the company/legal/contact/MVP proof package would materially improve the submission while preserving the current Tactivision.ai identity.
